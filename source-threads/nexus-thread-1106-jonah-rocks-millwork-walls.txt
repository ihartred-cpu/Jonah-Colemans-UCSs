Installation Instructions:
Open your UCS list and delete any of the listed UCS.  Doesn't seem like they properly overwrite.
CAB Before Build
Faces and Interiors (1/2)
Faces and Interiors (2/2)
FWP: DOR/DWR Overlays
FWP: Door/Drawer Fronts
Casework Walls ANYTHING (there may be up to 7 of these)
FIX FOR RADIUS BLANK PANELS NOT INSETTING
Import the package, selecting to overwrite existing.
Edit the new assembly material schedule called "CASEWORK WALL - PARTICLE BOARD".
Set all bandings to none.
Change all 3/4 particle boards to your 3/4 particle board material (you could use another size here).
Under interior parts, set "Casework Wall Cleat" to your 3/4 particle board (again you could use another size here).
Start a new job.
Place the "Casework Wall" object from the "Jonah Rocks" catalog.
Click through any error messages that pop up.  Don't worry about selecting schedules here, just accept the default.
Take the "Casework Wall" object to the assembly editor's section editor.
Edit the assembly's properties.
Set the Upper Standard Material Schedule to "CASEWORK WALL - PARTICLE BOARD".
Set the Hinge Schedule to the "CASEWORK WALL .75 DOORS" schedule.
Set Assembly Construction to CASEWORK WALL.
Save the assembly back into the catalog.  Do not change any check boxes for preserves.
Done.
 
You will edit the shape of the assembly from the plan view *ONLY*.  Anywhere you want panels is a face.  Anywhere you want a joint to another wall will be a "None", and anywhere you touch a wall and want a scribe is also a "None".
The most manual thing you will need to do is manually adjust the shape of your bottom plates for toe recess.
Suggested customizations: assign toolids for operations- search the UCS for TOOLID and you'll find commented out lines, along with a description of what kind of tool we use.  As packaged up I don't think there are any tool assignments.

* We use a 5mm *BRAD* point bit for the plate/cleat screw holes- as opposed to a v-point which results in a LOT of holes in your table.
* We use a 3/4" bit for slots when the material is close to but less than 3/4 (i.e. plywood).
* We use a 25/32" bit for slots when the material is 3/4" (i.e. particle board).
* We use a 3/16" up shear bit for the removable panel cutouts - the UCS is designed to force those parts face down to allow for this.  We have also used a toolset with a 1/2" bit to create a rabbet to make room for an 1/8" bit.

You can copy/paste face information from one face or assembly to another.  Take the control point to the part editor's report view and copy the single operation there to the clipboard.  Paste it onto another control point's part editor report view.  Then return to the assembly and cause a rebuild.  Pretty freaking cool, huh?
I'll try to come back later and make some more videos showing the more advanced items.
This UCS integrates tightly with my KEKU clips UCS and my miterfolding UCS, if you use those as well.
NOTE: On a radius wall, the radius shaped struts will not go to S2M with a shape.  I use another program to auto shape those, but you'll want to double click the part (you can select UCS added parts in v10 now), take it to the part editor, then copy its shape to the clipboard for use in S2M.  Or you can use that shape to create your own manual parts.

This file was sent to me by someone running into some common pitfalls in CV with radius items.
1) If the inside/outside faces are not truly, really, exactly concentric you get studs that aren't tangent to the arc.
To avoid this, never trust CAD for snapping.  Draw one face and use the offset tool to get the other one.  Then you KNOW they're concentric.
This applies to non-radius edges as well- if the inside/outside faces are not PRECISELY the same angle you get some funky behavior (infinitely long interiors, etc).
2) When placing studs inside radius faces, you get 1 shot.  After the first sectioning the new studs don't show up.
To avoid this, place all the studs you need on your first sectioning and then move them around.
3) Sometimes with radius faces, the inside or the outside are just terrible to work with.
Nothing to do here but use the other face.  It doesn't matter which face you put the studs in from.
4) With odd shapes, sometimes it doesn't identify the opposite face properly to get the machining from both faces on the studs.
You can override each face and specify which is the opposite face.
5) With odd shapes, sometimes studs run through multiple sections (not shown in this video)
Set the face priority of the section in between to 1 (or higher than both other ends).  This will stop it from running through once you add studs into it.
